# Culture Check calibration anchors — 2026-10-02

This note locks the editorial distinctions that matter most for the next hardened tranche. The goal is not to reward or punish a film for ordinary political disagreement. The goal is to identify the film's sustained cultural valence with evidence.

## Mandatory distinctions

1. **Criticism is not contempt.** Criticism of caste discrimination, a priest, a political party, an institution, a practice, a corrupt temple administration, or a specific religious actor does not by itself count as community contempt.
2. **Generalized ridicule does count.** Recurring caste/religious stereotypes, group-directed humiliation, slurs, or a narrative that repeatedly uses one community as the contempt object must be recorded in `community-contempt`.
3. **Politics is not religion.** Criticism of Hindutva, the BJP, Dravidian politics, Ambedkarite politics, secularism, or another political ideology is not automatically a religious-valence finding.
4. **Sacred asymmetry does matter.** A repeated narrative structure that makes Hindu deities, scripture, ritual, sacred figures, or symbols the object of ridicule, desecration, or one-directional moral inversion must be recorded in `sacred-religious-valence`, even when the filmmaker describes the intended target as political.
5. **Positive sacred representation also matters.** Devotion, deities, temple traditions, folklore, ritual, or civilizational memory can support certification when the film grants them dignity and narrative efficacy rather than merely using them as visual decoration.
6. **Counter-readings stay in the record.** A negative or positive verdict must retain the strongest good-faith counter-interpretation. The classifier may adjudicate it, but may not erase it.

## Calibration anchor: Karuppu (Tamil, 2026)

**Proposed verdict: Sanghi Certified.**

The film centres Karuppasamy/Karuppuswamy, a Tamil guardian deity, answering a devotee's prayer and taking human form to confront injustice. India Today describes the film as a devotional commercial film that *glorifies Karuppusaami and holds him high*; Indian Express describes a local deity entering a corrupt legal system to secure justice for ordinary people. This is not simply a fantasy device: faith, guardian-deity tradition, justice, protection and Tamil local rootedness are the film's moral architecture.

## Calibration anchor: A1: Accused No. 1 (Tamil, 2019)

**Proposed verdict: Reviewed · Not Certified.**

The inter-caste romance and criticism of caste prejudice are not the problem by themselves. The concern is the recurring caste-coded comic construction of Brahmin identity: exaggerated dialect, stereotypes around food and purity, and Brahmin characters functioning repeatedly as ridicule targets. The News Minute explicitly describes stereotypes on both sides and discusses the film's stripping of the 'highly cultured' mask around privileged Brahmins; India Today and Deccan Chronicle documented contemporary complaints that the film depicted the Brahmin community in a bad light. A negative Culture Check verdict therefore rests on generalized community treatment, not on the fact that the film criticises endogamy or casteism.

## Calibration anchor: Kaala (Tamil, 2018)

**Proposed verdict: Reviewed · Not Certified.**

The anti-caste and land-rights argument is not itself anti-Hindu and should not be penalized. The material negative signal is the film's sustained sacred/political symbolic architecture: several reviews identify the villain with Rama, the hero with Ravana, and the climax with a deliberate reversal in which Rama-coded power falls while Ravana multiplies. The Week describes a script in which Ram is killed and ten-headed Ravana rises repeatedly; Times of India states directly that 'Ram is bad and Ravan is good.' The strongest counter-reading, retained rather than suppressed, is that the film is attacking Hindutva/fascism rather than Hinduism; ThePrint makes that argument explicitly. Under Culture Check, the repeated Rama/Ravana and saffron-sacred asymmetry is nevertheless a material negative `sacred-religious-valence` finding.
