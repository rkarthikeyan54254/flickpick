# Hindu-centric lens clarification v2.1

Date: 2026-10-03
Status: binding clarification for Culture Check v2 evidence-derived certification

## Normative anchor

Culture Check is not a religion-neutral representational score. Its normative anchor is explicitly Bharatiya and Hindu-civilizational: Bharat-first national interest, Hindu sacred inheritance, Dharmic reasoning, civilizational continuity, Parampara, Itihasa, Raksha, rooted regional cultures and Social Dharma.

The evidence process remains even-handed about facts, but the cultural judgment is not required to be theologically neutral between religions. Equal civic dignity is a floor for treatment of people; it is not a rule that every religion, doctrine or practice must receive equal sacred protection or narrative balance.

## Non-Hindu doctrine and practice

A film is not downgraded merely because it depicts, criticises or uses a practice, doctrine or institution associated with Islam, Christianity or another non-Hindu religion. The audit must first ask what is actually shown and whether the depiction is factually supportable at the scope asserted.

In particular:

- depicting a practice that is doctrinally or legally permitted within another religion is not, by itself, a stereotype or community-contempt finding;
- criticism of a doctrine, practice, cleric, institution, proselytising method, extremist ideology or political movement is not community contempt merely because the subject is religious;
- a negative character who belongs to a non-Hindu religion does not create a community-contempt finding by identity alone;
- a film need not provide a balancing positive character from the same religion before a negative portrayal can be accepted;
- prevalence is a separate factual question. A film showing one permitted practice does not establish that most or all members of that religion follow it, and Culture Check must not silently convert a permitted practice into a population-wide claim.

## Hindu sacred-valence rule

The `sacred-religious-valence` probe is primarily a Hindu/Bharatiya sacred-regard probe. It tests how the film treats Hindu deities, temples, sacred geography, epics, rituals, gurus, symbols, festivals, inherited practices and related Bharatiya sacred material.

Negative portrayal of a non-Hindu doctrine or practice is not a negative sacred-valence finding merely because it is religious. If a film crosses into generalized degradation or dehumanization of people as a community, that belongs under the narrow `community-contempt` gate instead.

## Community-contempt remains narrow

The community-contempt gate protects people from generalized degradation; it does not impose secular representational symmetry.

A material community-contempt finding requires film-level evidence that the work generalizes inherent inferiority, collective guilt, ridicule, dehumanization or hatred to a community as a community. A practice-level observation, a villain's identity, asymmetric victim-centred storytelling or criticism of religious doctrine does not satisfy that threshold by itself.

## Evidence discipline does not change

A Hindu-centric lens does not permit unsupported claims about another community. Numerical prevalence, community-wide behaviour, coordinated conspiracy, conversion scale, demographic claims and historical attribution still require scale-matched evidence.

This distinction is mandatory:

- **Fact:** what the film, creator record or reliable sources establish.
- **Interpretation:** what those facts mean under the Hindu/Bharatiya lens.
- **Intent:** creator motive, asserted only when directly supported.

## Regression case: Animal (2023)

`Animal` must not receive a community-contempt or Narrative Integrity penalty merely because Abrar is written as a Muslim with multiple wives. Sandeep Reddy Vanga explicitly said that he used conversion to Islam in the character design partly because Islam permits multiple wives and because that enlarged the fictional family tree. That is a creator-confirmed plot-design choice.

The audit must not turn that fact into an unsupported claim that Muslim polygyny is a majority practice. The cultural verdict should instead be decided from the film's Bharatiya signals and Dharmic tensions: Punjabi/Sikh rootedness, kinship and lineage, the use of `Arjan Vailly`, protection of family, Indian self-reliance imagery, and the protagonist's serious moral disorder, violence and betrayal.

Therefore, the relevant contest is internal to the Hindu/Bharatiya lens — rootedness and Raksha versus Dharma and Social Dharma — not a requirement to neutralise the film because a Muslim antagonist practices polygyny.

## Publication rule

For all future evidence-derived profiles:

1. apply the Bharatiya/Hindu-civilizational lens before assigning cultural dimensions or verdict;
2. keep factual scrutiny rigorous and scale-matched;
3. do not treat non-Hindu doctrinal/practice criticism as a negative sacred-valence finding;
4. do not create a community-contempt finding from identity or a permitted practice alone;
5. do not manufacture interfaith representational balance;
6. keep genuine generalized community degradation as a blocking or contesting concern regardless of whether the film otherwise aligns with Bharatiya values.
