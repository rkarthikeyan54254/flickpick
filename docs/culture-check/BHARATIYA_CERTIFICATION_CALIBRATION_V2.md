# Bharatiya certification calibration v2

Date: 2026-10-02
Status: binding clarification for full-corpus re-certification

## Purpose

Culture Check is an explicitly Bharatiya, Hindu-civilizational certification system. It is not a secular-neutral review score and it does not require moral or representational symmetry between communities before a film can certify.

The certification question is whether the film materially aligns with Bharatiya/Hindu-civilizational values under the declared dimensions: Dharma, Civilizational Continuity, Rashtra, Itihasa, Parampara, Local Roots, Raksha, Social Dharma and Sacred Regard, while keeping Narrative Integrity as a separate evidence axis.

## Asymmetry is not itself disqualifying

A film is not downgraded merely because:

- its antagonists belong to a particular religion, caste, linguistic, regional or political community;
- it tells a story overwhelmingly from the victims' perspective;
- it depicts documented persecution, terrorism, separatism, conversion pressure, betrayal, state failure or communal violence without balancing every negative actor with a positive member of the same community;
- it makes an India-first, Hindu-civilizational, national-security or anti-colonial argument;
- a critic considers the film partisan, majoritarian, propagandistic or insufficiently secular.

Those claims may be relevant to Narrative Integrity or to an adversarial reading, but they do not replace the declared Bharatiya lens.

## Community-contempt threshold

The symmetric community-contempt gate remains binding, but its threshold is narrow and evidence-based.

Community contempt requires a repeated narrative pattern that generalizes inherent inferiority, ridicule, dehumanization, collective guilt or hatred to an identifiable community *as a community*, beyond what the film's depicted conflict and supported facts justify.

The following do **not** establish community contempt by themselves:

- showing terrorists, extremists, separatists, criminals, collaborators or persecutors negatively;
- showing that perpetrators in a historical episode predominantly came from one community;
- showing betrayal by neighbours or local collaborators when the episode is supported by testimony or historical evidence;
- failing to provide representational balance or a "good member" of the antagonist community;
- criticism of a doctrine, organisation, practice, political movement, cleric, caste hierarchy or institution;
- victim-centred storytelling.

A downgrade based on community contempt must therefore identify film-level evidence of generalized group degradation, not merely cite an ideological review that objects to asymmetry.

## Source weighting

Sources are used according to what they can establish:

1. Primary/official records, court material, archival evidence, participant testimony and creator/source records carry the greatest weight for factual claims.
2. Established reporting and serious historical/biographical work are used for corroboration and context.
3. Reviews and ideological commentary are useful for adversarial discovery and counter-reading, but a material certification downgrade must not rest mainly on one partisan or ideological interpretation.
4. A source's political orientation does not automatically invalidate factual evidence, but assertions must be separated from verifiable facts and corroborated when they materially affect a verdict.

## Narrative Integrity remains independent

A film may be Sanghi Certified while carrying strong Narrative Integrity caveats. Composite characters, compressed chronology, dramatized dialogue, disputed numerical claims, selective history or partisan framing must stay visible, but they reverse the cultural verdict only when they materially change the film's Bharatiya meaning or make the underlying positive claim unsupported.

## Regression example: The Kashmir Files

The historical reality of Kashmiri Pandit targeted killings, threats, displacement and civilizational loss may support strong Itihasa, Local Roots, Sacred Regard and Raksha/Rashtra signals. Islamist militants, separatists, collaborators or betraying neighbours may be portrayed negatively without that automatically becoming anti-Muslim community contempt.

The film must still carry factual caveats for composites, chronology compression and disputed scene-level claims. Community contempt should block certification only if the film itself demonstrably generalizes inherent guilt or inferiority to Muslims as Muslims beyond the documented conflict.

## Publication rule

For every v2 migration record:

- understand the film before verdicting;
- actively search for the strongest counter-reading;
- keep factual caveats visible;
- use adversarial sources to test the verdict, not to redefine the editorial lens;
- do not force secular representational balance into the certification standard;
- do not excuse genuine generalized contempt merely because the film otherwise aligns with Bharatiya values.
