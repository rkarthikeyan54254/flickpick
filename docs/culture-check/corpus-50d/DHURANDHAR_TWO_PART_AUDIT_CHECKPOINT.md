## 1. Dhurandhar (2025)

### Identity
- Title/year: `Dhurandhar` (2025)
- Director: Aditya Dhar
- Form: fictional spy-action thriller; not a Major Mohit Sharma biopic.

### Fact record
- Following a Delhi High Court-directed re-examination, the CBFC stated on 2025-12-02 that the film bears no direct or indirect resemblance to Major Mohit Sharma's life, service or experiences and is a work of fiction. The film carries a fiction disclaimer.
- The CBFC had viewed the film on 2025-11-28 and found it suitable for adult certification subject to modifications.
- This official finding controls the Mohit-Sharma attribution question for Narrative Integrity: the audit must not describe the film as his biopic or treat resemblance claims as established fact.

### Bharatiya/Hindu-civilizational interpretation to test
- Primary positive axis: Rashtra/Raksha through an Indian covert-operative frame confronting a hostile terror/criminal ecosystem.
- The audit must distinguish patriotic/national-security orientation from protagonist moral purity and from the brutality of the criminal milieu.
- Because the film is officially fictional rather than a biopic, historical parallels may be used only as contextual interpretation where independently supported; they cannot be presented as literal character identity.

### Sacred-valence / community-contempt gate
- No negative sacred-valence finding may be inferred merely from Muslim/Pakistani antagonist identity, Islamic setting, or depiction/criticism of extremist/criminal actors.
- A community-contempt downgrade requires film-level generalization of inherent guilt/inferiority to Muslims as Muslims, not asymmetry, villain identity, Pakistan-based antagonists, or victim/agent-centred storytelling alone.

### Strongest counter-reading / red team
- Test whether the film's fictionalization blurs recognizable real-world operations or people despite the disclaimer, and whether any promotional claim conflicts with the CBFC's formal finding.
- Test whether violence is merely genre/action grammar or is narratively endorsed in a way that materially degrades Dharma/Social Dharma.
- Test any alleged generalized community framing at scene/dialogue level rather than relying on ideological reviews.

### Fact / Interpretation / Intent discipline
- **Fact:** CBFC's formal re-examination found no Mohit Sharma link and classified the story as fictional.
- **Interpretation:** the film can still carry strong Rashtra/Raksha signals even when its central operative inhabits morally compromised covert/criminal spaces.
- **Intent:** do not infer Aditya Dhar's political or religious motive without direct creator evidence.

## 2. Dhurandhar: The Revenge (2026)

### Identity
- Title/year: `Dhurandhar: The Revenge` (2026), also marketed/referred to as `Dhurandhar 2`.
- Director: Aditya Dhar
- Release: 2026-03-19.
- Separate audit from the 2025 film; no franchise-inherited verdict.

### Fact record
- Contemporary reporting describes the sequel as following undercover Indian agent Jaskirat Singh Rangi / Hamza Ali Mazari inside Karachi's Lyari criminal-political underworld to dismantle a terror network.
- Action director Aejaz Gulab stated that the brutality was deliberately designed for the gangster/underworld story and that Aditya Dhar gave the action team broad freedom; the released violence was reportedly reduced from what was shot.
- Later television recertification reporting states the theatrical film had an adult certificate and that a shorter UA 16+ television version removed or reduced numerous violent scenes. This supports treating graphic violence as a real content caveat, not as a cultural-verdict shortcut.

### Bharatiya/Hindu-civilizational interpretation to test
- Primary positive axis to test: Rashtra/Raksha, covert duty and dismantling a terror network threatening India.
- The undercover identity and violent methods require a separate Dharma/Social-Dharma analysis. Under v2.1, moral compromise by the operative does not automatically reverse a film-level Bharatiya orientation.
- The sequel's more explicit political/national-security messaging must be evaluated from primary plot/creator evidence; critical labels such as `propaganda` are adversarial inputs, not decisive downgrade authority.

### Sacred-valence / community-contempt gate
- A mosque sequence, Muslim/Pakistani antagonist identity, or an Islamic social setting is not itself negative Hindu sacred valence and is not community contempt.
- Test the actual film for generalized degradation of Muslims as a community. Terrorist, gangster, extremist, corrupt-official and hostile-state actors can be depicted negatively without satisfying the narrow contempt threshold.

### Strongest counter-reading / red team
- Test whether extreme violence becomes the dominant endorsed civilizational ideal rather than an instrument of the depicted covert conflict.
- Test whether the sequel collapses Pakistani criminal/terror actors into generalized claims about Muslims or Pakistanis as inherently inferior; require scene/dialogue evidence.
- Test any history/current-affairs parallels for compression, invention or misleading attribution, keeping Narrative Integrity independent of the cultural verdict.
- International/ideological criticism describing the film as propagandistic should be used to locate claims for verification, not as verdict authority.

### Fact / Interpretation / Intent discipline
- **Fact:** the released sequel follows an Indian undercover agent in Lyari against a terror/criminal network; creator-team reporting confirms intentionally severe action design.
- **Interpretation:** those facts create potentially strong Rashtra/Raksha signals alongside material Dharma/Social-Dharma caveats from brutality and covert methods.
- **Intent:** creator political motive is not asserted from reviews; only direct statements may support intent.
