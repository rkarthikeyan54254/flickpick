## Dhurandhar (2025)

**Identity.** Hindi spy/action thriller directed by Aditya Dhar, released 5 December 2025. The story follows an Indian intelligence operative infiltrating Pakistan's underworld. It uses/evokes real India-Pakistan security events but is not a biopic of Major Mohit Sharma.

**Primary/creator clarification.** Aditya Dhar publicly and explicitly denied that the film is based on Major Mohit Sharma and said a future Mohit Sharma biopic would be made only with family consultation. This is binding for the real-person-attribution probe.

**Historical/source check.** Reporting around the film identifies real-event material including the IC-814 hijacking and 2001 Parliament attack. These historical anchors do not make the fictional undercover protagonist a literal historical person. Treat the work as historical/real-event fiction, not biography.

**Bharatiya/Hindu lens.** The film's central civilizational signal is Rashtra/Raksha: an Indian operative accepts extreme personal risk in service of Indian national security. Character brutality, deception and criminal immersion belong to protagonist-morality and Narrative Integrity analysis; they do not automatically negate film-level civilizational orientation.

**Sacred-valence check.** No researched evidence establishes ridicule/desecration of Hindu deity, scripture, temple, ritual or sacred symbol as a film-level thesis. Do not manufacture a sacred-regard penalty from absence of devotional material.

**Community-contempt check.** A legal notice alleged defamatory treatment of the Baloch community, specifically citing a dialogue that generalises distrust toward Baloch people. This is a genuine narrow community-contempt probe and must remain in the dossier. It is not equivalent to saying that Pakistani/Muslim antagonists themselves constitute community contempt.

**Strongest counter-reading.** The film can be read as nationalist mythmaking that folds real attacks and geopolitical history into a violent fictional espionage narrative, risking audience confusion about what is documented history versus invention; the Baloch dialogue creates an additional representation risk.

**Red-team outcome.** Qualified, not overturned. The counter-reading supports explicit historical/adaptation and community-representation caveats. It does not erase the film's India-centred Rashtra/Raksha orientation.

**Fact / Interpretation / Intent.** Fact: Dhar denied a Mohit Sharma biopic basis; the film is an Indian-undercover story incorporating real-event context, and a Baloch representation complaint was publicly reported. Interpretation: its film-level orientation is strongly India-security centred even though the operative's methods and milieu are morally dark. Intent: infer patriotic/security orientation from narrative structure and creator framing, but do not infer documentary truth or generalized hatred of a religious community without title-specific evidence.

**Provisional Culture Check:** Sanghi Certified, subject to final scoring and publication gate. Narrative Integrity should carry a history/fiction separation warning; community-contempt risk requires explicit qualification rather than automatic downgrade.
