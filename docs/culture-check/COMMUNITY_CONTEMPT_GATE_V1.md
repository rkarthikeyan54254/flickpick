# Community Contempt Gate v1

Date: 2026-10-02

## Purpose

Culture Check must not award a clean certification merely because a film has strong Rashtra, Dharma, sacred, historical or regional signals while overlooking material contempt directed at an Indian community.

This gate therefore runs independently of the positive Bharatiya dimensions.

## Communities covered

The screen is deliberately symmetric. It is not a protection rule for one preferred group.

Research must look for material treatment of:

- caste communities, including Brahmins, Dalits and other caste identities;
- Adivasi / tribal communities;
- Hindu, Muslim, Sikh, Christian, Jain, Buddhist and other religious communities;
- regional and linguistic communities;
- other clearly identifiable Indian social communities when the film makes them materially relevant.

The discovery pass explicitly searches for combinations of the film title with community identity plus terms such as insult, slur, stereotype, ridicule, contempt, representation and controversy.

## What counts as community contempt

A finding requires evidence that the film materially directs derogation at a community as a community. Relevant signals include:

- generalized slurs or demeaning labels;
- recurring stereotypes used as the community's defining trait;
- ridicule where group identity itself is the target rather than an individual character;
- asymmetrical treatment in which comparable conduct is moralized differently primarily because of community identity;
- repeated narrative framing that invites contempt toward the group rather than criticism of a specific action, practice or institution.

## What does not automatically count

The following are not sufficient on their own:

- criticism of caste hierarchy, patriarchy, corruption, extremism, clerical misconduct or another social practice;
- a villain or flawed character who belongs to a community;
- satire of one institution, leader or custom;
- historically documented conflict involving a community;
- disagreement with a religious or political claim;
- offensive dialogue spoken by a character when the film itself does not endorse the contemptuous framing.

Context, repetition, narrative endorsement and materiality matter.

## Publication rule

Every evidence-derived v2 dossier must contain the `community-contempt` research probe.

The publication gate applies these rules:

1. Missing community-contempt research or missing community-treatment discovery evidence puts the record on **provisional hold**.
2. For a proposed **Sanghi Certified** verdict, any **medium or high** community-contempt finding or unresolved ambiguity forces **human exception review**. It cannot auto-publish.
3. For Mixed / Contested, Neutral or other reviewed verdicts, a **high** community-contempt finding or ambiguity also forces human exception review.
4. Findings must point to evidence already present in the profile record.
5. A human approval may resolve the exception, but the underlying caveat remains visible in the evidence record; approval does not erase it.

## Relationship to other gates

This screen is separate from:

- `sacred-religious-valence`, which checks treatment of sacred and ritual material;
- `identity-substitution`, which checks whether identities were changed, hidden or reassigned;
- `social-radar`, which checks public factual and representation challenges;
- the overall `contemptRisk` editorial dimension.

A film can therefore have high Rashtra or Dharma scores and still fail to auto-certify because of material community contempt.

## Current-release implication

The same gate applies to the independent latest-release lane. A new release does not receive a final Sanghi Certified badge until this screen is present in its hardened research dossier. Trailer-only evidence is insufficient for a final verdict.
