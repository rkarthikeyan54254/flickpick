# 1. Kantara: Chapter 1 (2025)

**Language:** Kannada  
**Type:** Folk-mythological action / historical fantasy  
**Test:** Can the framework avoid mapping tribal resistance, anti-royal hierarchy and land protection to "Left" while missing the sacred/civilizational ontology?

## Dimension read

| Dimension | Score | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Dharma / Sacred Regard | **5/5** | Daivas and divine agency are real causal forces in the story, not merely superstition or metaphor. |
| Civilizational Continuity | **5/5** | Sacred landscape, ancestral memory, deities and community obligation structure the moral universe. |
| Rashtra | 1/5 | Not a modern national/political story; largely not applicable. |
| Itihasa / Historical Memory | 3/5 | Uses Kadamba-era setting and historical imagination, but is mythic/fantastical rather than a factual historical reconstruction. |
| Parampara | **5/5** | Ritual, inherited practice and intergenerational sacred responsibility are central. |
| Local Roots | **5/5** | Deeply rooted in coastal Karnataka tradition and local daiva practice. |
| Raksha | N/A | Not a modern security/service narrative. |
| Social Dharma | 4/5 | Exploitation and unjust power are condemned inside a sacred/local moral world rather than through an imported ideological frame. |

## Why v0.1 certifies it

The film's moral architecture is inseparable from sacred land, divine intervention, daivas, ritual and inherited community obligation. Rishab Shetty has explicitly said that even as the project scaled up, its cultural essence, tradition and values could not be compromised. He has also spoken against trivialising daiva ritual as viral content because it is sacred to the region's believers.

This is precisely where a generic political axis can mislead. MovieBias currently labels the film **Political: Leans Left** because tribal communities resist royal exploitation while simultaneously rating Hinduism positive. Our methodology treats anti-tyranny and protection of ancestral land as **not inherently Left or anti-civilizational**.

## Integrity findings

- `LOCAL_TRADITION_RESPECT`: **VERIFIED/SUPPORTED**, severity 0 (positive finding).
- `BROAD_HINDU_CONTEMPT`: **NOT_FOUND** in desk research.
- `CASTE_CRITICISM_AS_BLOCKER`: **REJECTED AS RULE** — criticism of hierarchy is not independently disqualifying.

## Provisional verdict

> 🪷 **SANGHI CERTIFIED — HIGH CONFIDENCE**

Strongest reasons: **Dharma, Civilizational Continuity, Parampara, Local Roots**.

# 2. Kalki 2898 AD (2024)

**Language:** Telugu  
**Type:** Mythological science fiction / dystopian epic  
**Test:** Does anti-authoritarian/anti-elite storytelling override a deeply Indic cosmological structure?

## Dimension read

| Dimension | Score | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Dharma / Sacred Regard | **5/5** | Kalki, Vishnu, Ashwatthama and dharmic/cosmic restoration are structural, not decorative. |
| Civilizational Continuity | **5/5** | Mahabharata time and future dystopia are presented as one civilizational continuum. |
| Rashtra | 1/5 | Modern Indian nationalism is not a primary axis. |
| Itihasa / Historical Memory | **5/5** | Kurukshetra/Mahabharata material directly anchors the futuristic narrative. |
| Parampara | 3/5 | Inherited epic/cosmological memory is strong; everyday social tradition is less central. |
| Local Roots | 4/5 | Telugu/Indian cinematic imagination consciously translates epic material into science fiction. |
| Raksha | N/A | Not a modern national-security narrative. |
| Social Dharma | 4/5 | Opposition to tyranny and extreme hierarchy functions alongside, not against, the dharmic cosmology. |

## Why v0.1 certifies it

Director Nag Ashwin has described the Mahabharata as universal across India and said the Kurukshetra opening was organic to the story; he notes that even remote parts of India have their own Mahabharata traditions. The film does not merely use Hindu iconography as set dressing — the prophecy of Kalki and Ashwatthama's continuity from the epic age are fundamental to the narrative.

MovieBias currently calls the film **Political: Strong Left** because it attacks dictatorship, elite power, inequality and caste discrimination, while separately calling Hinduism positive. v0.1 deliberately rejects the assumption that resistance to tyranny is inherently Western-progressive. A story can be anti-authoritarian while being deeply Indic in ontology and civilizational structure.

- `DHARMIC_ARCHITECTURE`: **VERIFIED/SUPPORTED**, strong positive finding.
- `ANTI_TYRANNY_AS_LEFT_BLOCKER`: **REJECTED AS RULE**.

## Provisional verdict

> 🪷 **SANGHI CERTIFIED — HIGH CONFIDENCE**

Strongest reasons: **Dharma, Civilizational Continuity, Itihasa**.

# 3. Amaran (2024)

**Language:** Tamil  
**Type:** Military biopic / romance  
**Test:** Can the framework certify through Rashtra/Raksha while handling a subtle allegation about omission of a real person's Brahmin/Iyengar identity without jumping from omission to malicious intent?

## Dimension read

| Dimension | Score | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Dharma / Sacred Regard | 2/5 | Hindu markers are present but not the film's core proposition. |
| Civilizational Continuity | 2/5 | Not a principal theme. |
| Rashtra | **5/5** | Indian identity, duty and sacrifice are central. |
| Itihasa / Historical Memory | 2/5 | Contemporary biographical record rather than civilizational history. |
| Parampara | 2/5 | Family and marriage are important but not strongly civilizational in function. |
| Local Roots | 4/5 | Tamil identity is intentionally emphasized alongside national identity. |
| Raksha | **5/5** | Army service, counterterrorism, sacrifice and military family experience form the core narrative. |
| Social Dharma | 3/5 | Interfaith marriage and identity are present without, on current evidence, displacing the national/service narrative. |

## Why v0.1 certifies it

Public reviews consistently describe *Amaran* as an ode to Major Mukund Varadarajan, Indian Army service, valour and sacrifice. The film therefore has a very strong Rashtra/Raksha certification route even though it is not principally devotional or civilizational.

## The Brahmin/Iyengar omission allegation

A factual point is established: Mukund Varadarajan belonged to the Iyengar community, and the film does not explicitly foreground his caste identity. However, director Rajkumar Periasamy publicly said Mukund's parents specifically asked that he be identified as **Indian and Tamilian**, reflecting how they said their son wished to be identified. Public reporting also notes that Mukund is shown as Hindu while Indhu is shown as Christian, with neither spouse's caste identified.

Therefore v0.1 records:

### Facts

- `MUKUND_IYENGAR_IDENTITY_OMITTED`: **VERIFIED**.
- `MUKUND_HINDU_IDENTITY_PRESENT`: **SUPPORTED** by reporting.
- `INDHU_CHRISTIAN_IDENTITY_PRESENT`: **VERIFIED/SUPPORTED**.
- `PARENTS_REQUESTED_INDIAN_TAMIL_IDENTITY`: **VERIFIED** as director's public account.

### Interpretation

- `RELIGIOUS_IDENTITY_ASYMMETRY`: **DISPUTED / NEEDS SCENE AUDIT**.

The question is not merely whether one identity can be named and another omitted; it is how much narrative/visual emphasis each receives and whether comparable information is selectively foregrounded. This requires direct scene/subtitle analysis.

### Intent

- `DELIBERATE_ANTI_BRAHMIN_INTENT`: **NOT ESTABLISHED**.

The family-request explanation is material counterevidence against inferring intent solely from omission.

This does **not** close the issue permanently. A scene audit could still establish meaningful representation asymmetry. It simply prevents the system from converting a plausible allegation into a fact before evidence supports it.

## Comparator lesson

MovieBias labels *Amaran* **Political: Strong Right** and **Islam: Negative**. v0.1 agrees that its nationalist/military orientation is strong, but would not infer hostility to Islam simply because terrorist antagonists are Muslim. It would require evidence of generalisation, identity-coded contempt or narrative treatment extending beyond specific militant characters.

## Provisional verdict

> 🪷 **SANGHI CERTIFIED — MEDIUM-HIGH CONFIDENCE**  
> ⚠ **Narrative Integrity: identity-asymmetry claim pending scene audit**

Strongest reasons: **Rashtra, Raksha, Local Roots**.

**Type:** Political / national-security thriller  
**Test:** Can worldview compatibility be separated from disputed factual/historical fidelity?

## Dimension read

| Dimension | Score | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Dharma / Sacred Regard | 1/5 | Not a central religious/civilizational story. |
| Civilizational Continuity | 2/5 | Some national historical framing, but not principally civilizational. |
| Rashtra | **5/5** | Sovereignty, Kashmir's constitutional integration and Indian state legitimacy are core. |
| Itihasa / Historical Memory | 3/5 | Historical-political claims are central but fact/fiction disputes reduce confidence. |
| Parampara | 1/5 | Not a primary dimension. |
| Local Roots | 2/5 | Kashmir is central, but critics dispute whether Kashmiri perspectives are represented with sufficient complexity. |
| Raksha | **5/5** | Intelligence, militancy, terrorism and state/security operations dominate the plot. |
| Social Dharma | 2/5 | Political/constitutional framing dominates over social-reform questions. |

## Why v0.1 certifies it

The film is unambiguously aligned with Indian sovereignty, national integration and state/security legitimacy. Under this framework it qualifies strongly through the **Rashtra/Raksha route**.

However, the badge cannot be allowed to imply that all historical claims in the film are therefore true.

## Narrative Integrity / source-fidelity dispute

The director has said his team aimed for authenticity and that a large portion of the film conveyed information not publicly known, claiming they remained close to facts. At the same time, multiple critics have argued that the film mixes facts with fiction, contains convenient inaccuracies and presents a strongly one-sided account of Kashmir politics.

v0.1 therefore records:

- `PRO_INDIA_INTEGRATION_FRAME`: **VERIFIED**, high confidence.
- `PRO_SECURITY_STATE_FRAME`: **VERIFIED**, high confidence.
- `SOURCE_FIDELITY_CONCERNS`: **SUPPORTED / DISPUTED**, severity 2–3 pending claim-by-claim audit.
- `ALL_HISTORICAL_CLAIMS_ACCURATE`: **NOT ESTABLISHED**.

Certification answers **worldview compatibility**. Narrative Integrity separately answers whether factual adaptation is reliable.

## Provisional verdict

> 🪷 **SANGHI CERTIFIED — MEDIUM-HIGH CONFIDENCE**  
> ⚠ **Narrative Integrity: historical/factual framing contested**

Strongest reasons: **Rashtra, Raksha**.

# 5. The Great Indian Kitchen (2021)

**Language:** Malayalam  
**Type:** Social drama  
**Test:** Can the framework distinguish criticism of patriarchy/religious practice from generalized anti-Hindu contempt, and can it return a negative certification without overclaiming hostility?

## Dimension read

| Dimension | Score | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Dharma / Sacred Regard | 1/5 | Ritual purity and Sabarimala-linked practice are substantially framed through their oppressive effect on the protagonist. |
| Civilizational Continuity | 1/5 | Inherited practices are primarily interrogated as mechanisms of unequal gender power rather than positively continued. |
| Rashtra | N/A | Not a national-identity story. |
| Itihasa / Historical Memory | N/A | Not a historical/epic story. |
| Parampara | 1/5 | Domestic and religious tradition is predominantly the object of critique. |
| Local Roots | **5/5** | Deeply grounded in Kerala household life, Sabarimala politics, menstruation practice and regional social context. |
| Raksha | N/A | Not applicable. |
| Social Dharma | **5/5** | The film is centrally concerned with women's dignity, unequal domestic labour and patriarchal restrictions. |

## Why v0.1 does not certify it

Director Jeo Baby has explicitly said the film is about women's rights and that he intentionally incorporated Sabarimala and menstrual-purity questions because these issues bothered him. The film's core dramatic structure therefore places inherited household/religious practices in sustained conflict with the protagonist's freedom and dignity.

Under the v0.1 lens this produces low Dharma/Parampara/Civilizational-Continuity alignment. That is sufficient for **Not Certified**.

But the framework must resist a second, opposite shortcut:

> `Criticises Sabarimala / menstruation restrictions -> anti-Hindu hatred`

That stronger claim is **not established by this desk pass**. The film targets specific practices and a patriarchal household. To label it broad Hindu contempt, the scene audit would need to show that Hindu identity/sacred life itself — rather than the contested practice — is repeatedly used as an object of ridicule or generalized moral inferiority.

## Integrity findings

- `CRITIQUE_OF_PATRIARCHAL_DOMESTIC_PRACTICE`: **VERIFIED**.
- `CRITIQUE_OF_SABARIMALA_RESTRICTION/PURITY`: **VERIFIED**.
- `BROAD_HINDU_CONTEMPT`: **UNVERIFIED / NOT ESTABLISHED**.
- `LOCAL_CULTURAL_GROUNDING`: **VERIFIED**, strong.

This is an important negative control: a movie can be **Not Sanghi Certified without being labelled anti-Hindu**.

## Provisional verdict

> **NOT SANGHI CERTIFIED — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE**

Reason: its central relationship to inherited Hindu domestic/religious practice is critical rather than affirming; the negative certification does **not** imply an accusation of hatred or propaganda.

# What the five-film pilot teaches us

## 1. The Western Left/Right axis is demonstrably insufficient

*Kantara: Chapter 1* and *Kalki 2898 AD* are the strongest examples. Anti-tyranny, anti-exploitation and social-equity themes coexist with a sacred/Indic ontology. Treating the first set of themes as primary and the latter as a secondary "religion" field misses what the stories fundamentally are.

## 2. Certification needs multiple routes

- *Kantara* certifies through **Dharma + Parampara + Local Roots + Civilizational Continuity**.
- *Kalki* certifies through **Dharma + Itihasa + Civilizational Continuity**.
- *Amaran* certifies through **Rashtra + Raksha**.
- *Article 370* certifies through **Rashtra + Raksha**, while carrying a factuality warning.

One ideological template would fail these differences.

## 3. Narrative Integrity must stay separate

A certification badge must never mean:

- every historical claim is accurate;
- every representation choice is fair;
- every scene is endorsed;
- there are no disputed omissions.

*Amaran* and *Article 370* make this separation essential.

## 4. The Facts / Interpretation / Intent split works

The *Amaran* controversy shows why:

- **Fact:** caste identity omitted.
- **Interpretation:** religious/community emphasis may be asymmetric.
- **Intent:** anti-Brahmin motive is not established; the director cites a family request.

Without these layers, the classifier would simply reproduce partisan allegations as facts.

## 5. Social reform is not automatically a negative signal

The framework must ask **what worldview conducts the reform**. *Kantara* criticises exploitation without ceasing to be sacred/civilizational. *The Great Indian Kitchen* is a different case because inherited practice itself is a major object of critique, but even there the framework should not leap to "anti-Hindu hatred" without evidence.

## 6. We need scene evidence before scaling

Desk research is sufficient to validate the shape of the methodology, but not sufficient for robust forensic flags at scale. The next technical milestone should ingest legally available subtitle/transcript material and allow findings to carry timestamps.

## 7. We should not publish a 0–100 score yet

The five films show that dimensions have different applicability by genre. A single averaged number would create false precision. Keep dimension scores internally and publish the categorical badge + reasons + confidence until the gold set is large enough to calibrate a stable index.

# Pilot decision

**Hypothesis status: SUPPORTED.**

The v0.1 model yields coherent differentiation across five languages and very different film types while avoiding three failure modes:

1. Western political-axis reduction.
2. Automatic acceptance of nationalist films as historically accurate.
3. Automatic treatment of social criticism as anti-Hindu hostility.

## Recommended next step

Before UI work, expand this to a **30-film gold calibration set**:

- 10 expected Certified / obvious positive anchors.
- 10 expected Not Certified / negative anchors.
- 10 difficult Mixed/Contested cases selected specifically to break the methodology.

For every contested case, require at least one scene-level/timestamped evidence item before final adjudication.
