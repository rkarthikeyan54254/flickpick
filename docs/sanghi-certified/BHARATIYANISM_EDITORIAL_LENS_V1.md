# Bharatiyanism Editorial Lens v1

**Status:** Binding editorial lens for Sanghi Certified classification  
**Applies to:** Batch 02 onward; existing calibration corpus must be re-read against this lens before hardening  
**Relationship to methodology:** This document defines *what lens we are applying*. MASTER_PLAN_V0.3 and EDITORIAL_PUBLICATION_GATE_V1 define *how evidence is gathered and adjudicated*.

## 1. Core proposition

Sanghi Certified is not a generic Left/Right classifier and not an exercise in performative centrism.

It evaluates cinema from an explicitly **Bharatiya, India-first, Hindu-civilizational lens**.

The central editorial question is:

> When this film is read from within Bharat — its civilizational memory, Hindu traditions, national interest, sovereignty, regional cultures, historical experience and social realities — is the film broadly aligned with, respectful of, or corrosive toward that inheritance and interest?

The lens may take a position. It must still distinguish position from evidence.

## 2. Bharatiyanism — declared editorial commitments

### 2.1 Bharat first

- India’s sovereignty, territorial integrity and constitutional unity are foundational positives.
- National-security threats, terrorism, foreign aggression, separatism and coercive external influence may be evaluated from India’s side rather than through mandatory narrative symmetry.
- Indian achievement in science, defence, infrastructure, culture, sport, enterprise or state capacity is not treated with reflexive suspicion merely because it is national achievement.
- India-first does not mean state-worship: corruption, incompetence, abuse of power and injustice may be criticised without becoming anti-national.

### 2.2 Hindu civilizational continuity

- Hindu temples, sacred geography, deities, rituals, festivals, gurus, mathas, pilgrimage, family practices and inherited traditions are legitimate living civilizational institutions, not merely superstition or aesthetic props.
- Films receive positive alignment when they treat this inheritance with seriousness, affection, continuity or internal reform rather than contempt.
- Temple protection, restoration, civilizational memory and reclamation of heritage are legitimate Bharatiya concerns.

### 2.3 Hindu unity without defending hierarchy

- The lens does not treat caste hierarchy or birth-based humiliation as something that must be defended in order to defend Hindu civilization.
- Criticism of caste injustice is not automatically anti-Hindu.
- A film may be strongly Bharatiya while attacking discrimination, untouchability, exclusion or inherited social abuse.
- The key distinction is between **reform within/for the civilization** and **using injustice as shorthand for contempt toward the civilization itself**.

### 2.4 Conversion and coercion vigilance

- Religious conversion, coercive proselytization, deception, radicalisation and recruitment into extremist organisations are legitimate subjects of scrutiny.
- The lens does not require false equivalence when evidence shows one specific actor, network or ideology responsible for a specific case.
- Identity concealment, coercion, manipulation, inducement, forced conversion and recruitment should be surfaced when supported by evidence.
- At the same time, a claim about a specific network, number, community-wide pattern or coordinated conspiracy must be supported at the scale being asserted. Concern about a real phenomenon does not convert an unsupported statistic into a verified fact.

### 2.5 Historical consciousness

- Invasion, colonisation, temple destruction, religious persecution, partition, displacement, resistance, civilizational loss and recovery are legitimate parts of Indian historical memory.
- The classifier must not erase those subjects merely to avoid discomfort or controversy.
- Conversely, specific historical claims — dates, numbers, named actors, incidents, motives — remain subject to source verification.

### 2.6 Equal civic dignity without performative secularism

- Bharatiyanism does not require hostility toward Muslims, Christians or other communities.
- Individuals and communities should not be assigned collective guilt from isolated conduct.
- Equal civic dignity does not require pretending that every conflict has symmetrical facts, motives or responsibility.
- The methodology must be willing to identify anti-Hindu ridicule, anti-Brahmin caricature, Islamist extremism, Christian coercive proselytization, state abuse, caste oppression or any other supported pattern without artificially balancing the finding with an unrelated counterexample.

### 2.7 Representation fairness

The audit must actively look for:

- selective disclosure of religion/caste/community identity
- identity removal from positive real-life figures while retaining another group’s identity
- invented identity markers for villains or comic characters
- transfer of blame from a documented real-world actor to a different community-coded fictional actor
- unequal sacred treatment across religions
- Hindu/Brahmin/temple/ritual markers used as shorthand for stupidity, corruption, backwardness or villainy
- national symbols, soldiers or Indian institutions caricatured merely to signal sophistication or moral superiority

A single negative character is not enough. Repeated narrative coding or source-vs-film substitution matters.

## 3. Certification meaning

A `Sanghi Certified` verdict means:

> From the declared Bharatiya lens, the film’s dominant treatment of Bharat, Hindu civilization, national interest, sacred inheritance, historical memory or rooted Indian social life is substantially compatible with the lens.

Certification does **not** mean:

- every factual assertion is correct
- every Hindu character is good
- every Muslim/Christian character is bad
- the film is pro-government
- the film is socially conservative on every question
- criticism of caste/patriarchy/state institutions is disqualifying
- all Narrative Integrity concerns are absent

## 4. Narrative Integrity remains independent

Narrative Integrity answers a different question:

> How faithfully and transparently does the film handle real people, events, identities, scale claims and source material?

A film may therefore be:

- **Sanghi Certified + clean integrity record**
- **Sanghi Certified + factual/adaptation warning**
- **Mixed + integrity warning**
- **Not Certified + clean factual record**

A factual-integrity failure does not automatically revoke certification unless the factual manipulation itself materially changes the film’s Bharatiya/civilizational meaning.

## 5. Evidence discipline — non-negotiable

Taking a Bharatiya position does not require lowering the evidentiary bar.

Always separate:

### Fact
What is established by film evidence, primary material, official records or reliable attributable sourcing.

### Interpretation
What the established facts reasonably imply about representation, civilizational meaning or narrative asymmetry.

### Intent
Why the filmmaker made the choice. Intent must not be asserted without unusually strong evidence.

Particularly high-risk claims requiring scale-matched evidence include:

- numerical prevalence claims
- claims of a coordinated conspiracy/network
- claims assigning conduct to an entire religious/community population
- claims of deliberate hatred or propaganda intent
- historical casualty/conversion/destruction counts

## 6. No false neutrality rule

The classifier is not required to manufacture a midpoint between competing narratives.

Examples:

- India’s territorial claim does not need to be weakened because a foreign state contests it.
- A verified Islamist terror case does not need an unrelated Hindu-extremism counterexample for balance.
- A verified anti-Hindu caricature does not need an unrelated anti-Muslim scene for balance.
- A caste-injustice story does not need a pro-tradition counter-scene to be treated fairly.

What is required is **evidence, scope discipline and accurate labeling of uncertainty**.

## 7. Kerala Story regression rule

*The Kerala Story* is a permanent regression case for separating Bharatiya alignment from factual scale.

- Documented forced-conversion/ISIS-radicalisation allegations and cases are legitimate subjects of Bharatiya security concern.
- The film’s treatment of extremist recruitment can therefore align strongly with the Bharatiya/Raksha lens.
- The specific `32,000` promotional claim remains a separate quantitative-source question. If that number is unsupported at that scale, it is recorded as a Narrative Integrity finding without erasing the underlying security concern or automatically revoking certification.

## 8. Moat

The moat is not political correctness, nor contrarianism, nor the badge itself.

It is:

> A culturally literate Bharatiya judgment that is willing to take a position — and disciplined enough to show exactly which parts are fact, which parts are interpretation, what evidence supports them, and where the evidence stops.
