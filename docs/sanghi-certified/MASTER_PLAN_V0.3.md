# Sanghi Certified — Master Plan v0.3

**Project:** FlickPick  
**Status:** Editorial methodology — supersedes v0.2  
**Purpose:** Define an India-grounded, evidence-backed certification system that explains every verdict and actively searches for subtle narrative choices before publication.

## 1. Product promise

Sanghi Certified is not a quality rating and not a generic Left/Right classifier. It answers:

> Before I invest my time, how does this film treat India, Indic civilization, dharma, inherited traditions, history, sovereignty and culturally sensitive representation — and what evidence explains that conclusion?

A verdict without an explanation is an incomplete product result.

## 2. Declared editorial baseline

The methodology is explicitly India-grounded.

- Indian sovereignty, constitutional unity and territorial integrity are positive baseline values.
- Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh are evaluated from India’s constitutional/state position that they are integral parts of India.
- A film is not penalized for presenting India’s national-security or sovereignty perspective without balancing it against a competing foreign territorial claim.
- Worldview alignment and factual accuracy remain separate questions.

## 3. Mandatory public outputs

Every reviewed film must expose, in the product UI:

1. **Verdict** — Certified / Mixed / Neutral / Not Certified.
2. **Confidence** — High / Medium / Low.
3. **Why this result** — at least two concrete reasons.
4. **Signal map** — the relevant editorial dimensions.
5. **Narrative Integrity** — material adaptation, identity, representation or source-fidelity findings, including a clear "none recorded" state.
6. **Evidence trail** — source notes sufficient for users to understand what drove the review.
7. **Review depth** — calibration/desk, source-audit or scene-audit.

The shuffle card must show a concise explanation. Explanation must not be available only after navigating to a detail page.

## 4. Publication gate: no passive classification

A movie must not receive a durable reviewed verdict merely because ordinary plot/review research found no contrary evidence.

Before publication, the reviewer/classifier must actively attempt to falsify its own first impression.

### 4.1 Adversarial discovery pass — mandatory

For every film, search for:

- religious/caste/community caricature or ridicule
- identity asymmetry
- hero/villain identity patterns
- one-off dialogue or visual symbols missed by plot summaries
- regional-language criticism and praise
- creator interviews explaining contested choices
- X/Twitter, Reddit and YouTube claims containing timestamps, screenshots, clips or before/after comparisons
- allegations of name, caste, religion, community, event or symbol substitution

Social media is radar, never verdict. A viral allegation remains one claim until independently checked.

### 4.2 True-story / historical / biographical pass — mandatory

Any film marketed as true, inspired by real events, biographical or historical must receive an adaptation-delta audit before its verdict is treated as reviewed rather than provisional.

Compare source record with film portrayal for:

- name changes
- caste/community/religious identity omitted, added or transferred
- ideological affiliation inserted or removed
- marriage/ritual changed
- political or social symbolism inserted
- victim/perpetrator identity changes
- chronology and event inversion
- invented composites
- selective retention of one group’s identity while removing another’s

A change is not automatically evidence of prejudice. It is, however, evidence that requires interpretation.

## 5. Facts / Interpretation / Intent — mandatory separation

Every sensitive Narrative Integrity finding must distinguish:

### Fact
What is independently established.

### Interpretation
What the fact plausibly means in the narrative or representation pattern.

### Intent
What can actually be established about why the filmmaker made the choice.

Do not jump from a verified adaptation difference directly to a claim of hostile intent.

## 6. Rules that prevent inverse-bias classification

The following are explicit guardrails:

- caste criticism is not automatically anti-Hindu
- criticism of a Brahmin character is not automatically anti-Brahmin
- criticism of police/state institutions is not automatically anti-national
- anti-tyranny themes are not automatically Left/progressive
- female agency is not automatically progressive/anti-traditional
- presence of a Muslim or Christian hero is not automatically disqualifying
- presence of a Hindu/Brahmin villain is not automatically evidence of contempt
- the relevant question is whether identity itself is weaponized as shorthand for ridicule, villainy or moral inferiority
- social reform can occur inside an Indic/civilizational frame

## 7. Neutral requires evidence too

`Neutral` must not mean "we did not look deeply enough to find a signal."

A durable Neutral verdict requires confidence that:

- the film does not materially engage the certification dimensions, or
- competing signals are genuinely weak rather than merely uninvestigated.

If meaningful conflicting signals exist, use `Mixed / Contested`.

If the audit is incomplete, the product should expose the record as a calibration/provisional review rather than implying a final neutral conclusion.

## 8. Narrative Integrity statuses

Findings use:

- `VERIFIED`
- `SUPPORTED`
- `DISPUTED`
- `UNVERIFIED`
- `CONTRADICTED`

A film can be Sanghi Certified while carrying integrity findings. Certification is worldview alignment, not a guarantee that every factual claim in the film is true.

## 9. Versioned editorial revisions

Editorial judgments are versioned. Evidence-backed changes do not silently erase the initial classification.

The product should prefer the latest revision while retaining earlier calibration records in source control.

Each revision records:

- methodology version
- review date
- confidence
- review depth
- reasons for the current verdict
- Narrative Integrity findings
- evidence trail

## 10. Worked regression case — Jai Bhim

The initial pilot classified *Jai Bhim* as Neutral because its central narrative concerns custodial abuse, constitutional remedy and justice for a marginalised community, and the first pass did not establish broad anti-Hindu contempt.

That first-pass Neutral result is now treated as a methodology failure because it was published before a mandatory adaptation-delta audit.

A deeper source pass surfaced material changes around the real case, including community substitution and perpetrator identity/name changes, plus a disputed community-symbolism controversy. These findings do not establish blanket anti-Hindu intent, but they make a simple Neutral verdict inadequate.

The current v0.3 result is therefore `Mixed / Contested`, with Fact / Interpretation / Intent explicitly separated.

This case is a permanent regression test: future true-story films must be checked for equivalent adaptation deltas before a durable verdict is published.

## 11. Scaling rule

As the corpus grows from 30 -> 100 -> 500+, automation may gather sources and propose findings, but publication gates do not weaken.

High-risk films are automatically routed to deeper review when they are:

- historical / biographical / true-story adaptations
- politically or communally contested
- flagged by social-media claims containing falsifiable scene evidence
- low-confidence or internally contradictory
- showing identity or symbolic asymmetries

Easy films can move faster. Difficult films must move deeper.

## 12. Product principle

The moat is not the badge itself.

The moat is the ability to say:

> Here is the verdict. Here is why. Here is what changed from the source record. Here is what we know, what we infer, and what we cannot prove.

That standard is the backbone of Sanghi Certified.
