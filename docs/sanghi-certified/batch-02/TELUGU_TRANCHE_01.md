## 21. Balagam (2023) — Sanghi Certified — HIGH — reviewed

**Dimensions:** Dharma 4 · Civilization 5 · Rashtra 1 · Itihasa 2 · Parampara 5 · Local Roots 5 · Raksha 1 · Social Dharma 4 · Sacred Regard 4 · Contempt Risk 0.

**Why this result**
- The film makes Telangana death rites, kinship obligations, Budaga Jangam mourning songs, shraddha/crow ritual and village social life the actual dramatic grammar rather than decorative folklore.
- Its moral resolution is reconciliation inside inherited community practice: ritual becomes a means through which a fractured family confronts unresolved relationships.
- Adversarial search found a story-origin allegation, but director Venu Yeldandi publicly described the autobiographical family/death-ritual experiences from which he developed the film and denied copying the complainant's story; available evidence does not establish plagiarism.

**Narrative Integrity**
- `story-origin-dispute` — DISPUTED. **Fact:** a writer publicly alleged similarity; Yeldandi denied copying and described concrete family experiences behind the script. **Interpretation:** originality was contested after release, but the attributable record presently supports recording the dispute rather than declaring appropriation. **Intent:** no dishonest intent established.

## 22. Virupaksha (2023) — Mixed / Contested — MEDIUM — reviewed

**Dimensions:** Dharma 2 · Civilization 3 · Rashtra 1 · Itihasa 1 · Parampara 3 · Local Roots 4 · Raksha 1 · Social Dharma 3 · Sacred Regard 2 · Contempt Risk 2.

**Why this result**
- The film is deeply rooted in a Telugu village-deity/festival setting, but it deliberately puts inherited occult belief, priestly prescriptions and collective superstition under suspicion.
- Its opening establishes lethal mob punishment of people accused of witchcraft; later the narrative also makes supernatural/black-magic forces real inside the story. That produces a genuinely mixed signal rather than a simple pro- or anti-tradition verdict.
- The protagonist's investigative/rational posture contests harmful credulity without turning every sacred element into ridicule.

**Narrative Integrity**
- `belief-system-ambiguity` — VERIFIED. **Fact:** reviews consistently describe both the village-deity ritual framework and the plot's critique of superstition/witchcraft accusation, while the fictional curse/occult mechanism is ultimately real within the genre. **Interpretation:** the film distinguishes harmful social superstition imperfectly because supernatural causation is also validated. **Intent:** genre horror intent is clear; broad anti-Hindu intent is not established.

## 23. Dasara (2023) — Mixed / Contested — HIGH — reviewed

**Dimensions:** Dharma 2 · Civilization 3 · Rashtra 1 · Itihasa 1 · Parampara 2 · Local Roots 5 · Raksha 1 · Social Dharma 5 · Sacred Regard 2 · Contempt Risk 1.

**Why this result**
- The film is exceptionally rooted in Telangana dialect, coal-belt landscape and village culture; its locality is substantive rather than generic production design.
- Caste exclusion is materially built into the village's central Silk Bar: upper-caste men drink inside while lower-caste men remain outside. The film treats this hierarchy critically; under v0.3, caste criticism is not automatically anti-Indic.
- Alcohol, local power and caste politics are shown as socially corrosive, while friendship, loyalty and protection drive the moral center. These competing signals justify Mixed rather than Neutral or Not Certified.

**Narrative Integrity**
- `caste-representation` — VERIFIED. **Fact:** multiple reviews independently describe caste segregation at the bar and village power structure. **Interpretation:** caste hierarchy is not incidental background; it is one mechanism of exclusion in the film's social world. **Intent:** criticism of caste power is evident; a broader claim of civilizational contempt is not supported.

## 24. Major (2022) — Sanghi Certified — HIGH — reviewed

**Dimensions:** Dharma 3 · Civilization 3 · Rashtra 5 · Itihasa 4 · Parampara 2 · Local Roots 3 · Raksha 5 · Social Dharma 4 · Sacred Regard 2 · Contempt Risk 0.

**Why this result**
- The film centers duty, sacrifice and protection of civilians through Major Sandeep Unnikrishnan's life and death during the 26/11 Mumbai attacks; its national-service signal is direct and sustained.
- The filmmakers deliberately avoided chest-thumping hostility and drew extensively on Sandeep's parents, teachers, friends and coursemates, allowing patriotism to be expressed through character and duty rather than communal hatred.
- It remains a dramatized biopic: childhood incidents are composites, the final moments are reconstructed, and a review notes substantial creative freedom and unrealistic military staging. Certification therefore does not imply documentary fidelity.

**Narrative Integrity**
- `biographical-compression` — VERIFIED. **Fact:** director Sashi Kiran Tikka says several childhood moments consolidate multiple real events and that the ending/final moments are dramatized; Sandeep's father also warned before release that no biopic could depict him 100%. **Interpretation:** the emotional portrait is family-sourced but some scenes are composites/reconstructions. **Intent:** filmmakers openly acknowledge dramatization rather than presenting every scene as literal record.
- `military-staging` — SUPPORTED. **Fact:** Indian Express's review criticizes the training and Taj-operation staging as departing from military hierarchy/realism. **Interpretation:** this affects factual texture, not the underlying fact of Sandeep's service/sacrifice. **Intent:** cinematic simplification is plausible; deceptive intent not established.

**Evidence trail**

## 25. Sita Ramam (2022) — Mixed / Contested — HIGH — reviewed

**Dimensions:** Dharma 3 · Civilization 3 · Rashtra 4 · Itihasa 2 · Parampara 3 · Local Roots 3 · Raksha 4 · Social Dharma 5 · Sacred Regard 3 · Contempt Risk 1.

**Why this result**
- Lieutenant Ram is framed as an Indian soldier of integrity who protects Kashmiri civilians and remains duty-bound; the story clearly recognizes Pakistani infiltration and anti-India conditioning rather than flattening the conflict into moral equivalence.
- At the same time, its central ethical argument is that humanity can cross religion and national borders. Afreen's arc moves from reflexive hostility toward India to recognition of Ram's sacrifice, while Ramayana names/roles provide a deliberate Indic narrative echo.
- The film also takes conspicuous liberties with military logic and procedure; these are fidelity caveats, not enough to erase its India-positive and inter-community reconciliation signals.

**Narrative Integrity**
- `military-procedure-fiction` — SUPPORTED. **Fact:** contemporary criticism identifies implausible treatment of the Army's fugitive/deserter status and other military logic. **Interpretation:** the political/military setting is romantic melodrama rather than reliable historical procedure. **Intent:** dramatic convenience is evident; misinformation intent is not established.
- `religion-nationality-framing` — VERIFIED. **Fact:** Afreen begins as a Pakistan-origin India-hating character described in one regional review as intensely religious, and her arc explicitly changes through learning Ram's story. **Interpretation:** the film criticizes hatred/conditioning, not Islam as a whole, and also shows humane Pakistani characters. **Intent:** reconciliation/humanity-over-hatred is supported by the completed arc.
