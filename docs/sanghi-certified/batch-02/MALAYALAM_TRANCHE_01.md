## 32. Bramayugam (2024) — Mixed / Contested — REVIEWED
Confidence: high · reviewDepth: source-audit · auditStatus: reviewed

Dimensions: Dharma 2 · Civilization 4 · Rashtra 1 · Itihasa 3 · Parampara 4 · Local Roots 5 · Raksha 1 · Social Dharma 4 · Sacred Regard 2 · Contempt Risk 3.

Why:
- The film is deeply rooted in Kerala folk cosmology—Chaathan, Varahi, mana/illam, ritual and caste hierarchy are story architecture rather than decorative references.
- Its politics of domination explicitly connect Brahmin authority, caste and inherited power with exploitation, while the supernatural world itself is treated as narratively real. That creates a genuine mixed signal: strong local/civilizational rootedness alongside a harsh critique of a Brahmin power structure.
- A real Namboodiri household objected that the original character name resembled its lineage and linked it to black magic; the makers changed the name before release. That is a verified representation intervention, but does not establish anti-Brahmin intent.

Narrative Integrity finding — identity/name association — VERIFIED.
Fact: The lead was originally named Kunjumon/Kunjamon Potti; Punjamon Illam approached the Kerala High Court alleging reputational harm from association with a black-magic character; the makers changed the name to Kodumon Potti and CBFC accepted the change.
Interpretation: The pre-release naming created a sufficiently concrete resemblance to a living/traditional Brahmin household that it warranted correction; users should see this rather than only a generic caste label.
Intent: Deliberate defamation or hostility toward that family/Brahmins is not established by the available evidence.

Evidence:

## 33. Aadujeevitham / The Goat Life (2024) — Mixed / Contested — REVIEWED
Confidence: high · reviewDepth: source-audit · auditStatus: reviewed

Dimensions: Dharma 2 · Civilization 2 · Rashtra 1 · Itihasa 1 · Parampara 2 · Local Roots 4 · Raksha 1 · Social Dharma 5 · Sacred Regard 2 · Contempt Risk 1.

Why:
- The central moral signal is human dignity under modern slavery, endurance and longing for home; it is strongly Kerala-rooted but not chiefly about Indian national/civilizational identity.
- It is a two-step adaptation: film from Benyamin's novel, which itself fictionalises a real Najeeb experience. The lead actor and director openly distinguish the real Najeeb from the novel's and film's Najeeb.
- Because users may encounter it as a 'true story,' the adaptation distance is material enough to keep the result Mixed rather than presenting a simple factual-life account.

Narrative Integrity — biographical adaptation distance — VERIFIED.
Fact: Prithviraj said there are substantial differences between the real Najeeb and the character he/ Blessy constructed; Blessy has described the film as his version of the novel and made selective changes for cinema.
Interpretation: The emotional truth may be grounded in Najeeb's ordeal, but individual scenes/character details should not automatically be treated as documentary facts.
Intent: The filmmakers explicitly frame these as artistic adaptation choices; deceptive intent is not established.

- A strongly regional friendship/survival story based on the 2006 Guna Caves rescue; loyalty, courage and perseverance drive the film more than ideological or religious claims.
- One analysis explicitly notes that Subhash's atheism is a plot point while the film builds a broader idea of faith through friendship rather than religion. That is not equivalent to anti-religious contempt.

Interpretation: The rescue's broad factual spine is real, while staging and physical geography are cinematic reconstruction.
Intent: No evidence found of identity substitution or ideological alteration of the rescue.
