**Audit status:** reviewed

**Dimensions:** Dharma 4 · Civilization 4 · Rashtra 2 · Itihasa 3 · Parampara 4 · Local Roots 5 · Raksha 1 · Social Dharma 3 · Sacred Regard 3 · Contempt Risk 0

**Why**
- The film treats kinship, ancestral home, rural Tamil memory, reconciliation and inherited belonging as sources of moral repair rather than embarrassment.
- Its long conversations invoke Tamil historical/political memory and jallikattu inside a deeply local family story; the strongest signal is cultural rootedness rather than party politics.
- An adversarial pass found a substantive critique that the film's nostalgic village gaze can understate caste exclusion. That is a useful interpretive counterweight, but not evidence that the film weaponizes caste/religious identity or expresses civilizational contempt.

This is a perspective on what the film leaves out, not proof of a hidden factual substitution.

**Interpretation:** The film is unusually affirmative about Tamil rootedness and inherited relationships. The omission critique lowers confidence but does not reverse the dominant signal.  
**Intent:** No evidence found that the creators intended caste erasure; intent remains unestablished.

## 17. Vaazhai (2024)

**Hypothesis:** Mixed / Contested  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Review depth:** source-audit  
**Audit status:** reviewed

**Dimensions:** Dharma 3 · Civilization 3 · Rashtra 1 · Itihasa 1 · Parampara 2 · Local Roots 5 · Raksha 1 · Social Dharma 5 · Sacred Regard 2 · Contempt Risk 1

**Why**
- Mari Selvaraj describes the film as autobiographical; its moral center is the exploitation of poor banana-plantation workers and the stolen childhood of a rural Tamil boy.
- Caste, class and labour exploitation are explicit, but criticism of hierarchy is not by itself anti-Indic; the film remains intensely rooted in local speech, landscape, family and community.
- A named writer, Cho Dharman, publicly noted substantial surface similarities with his earlier short story `Vaazhaiyadi`; his own statement also identifies several important elements present in the film but absent from his story. The available evidence supports recording the provenance dispute without declaring plagiarism proven.

**Narrative Integrity:** `source-similarity-dispute`, status `disputed`. Cho Dharman said his decade-old story also concerned banana-load workers, lorry/driver/middleman/owner, children, hardship and wages, while noting that the film adds teacher/handkerchief/communist-symbol/accident material. Mari Selvaraj publicly pointed readers to Dharman's story. No adjudication establishing copying was located.

**Fact:** Selvaraj has called *Vaazhai* his biopic; attributable reporting records Dharman's similarity claim and his list of differences.  
**Interpretation:** The autobiographical basis and prior literary similarity can coexist; provenance should be visible because certainty would overstate the evidence.  
**Intent:** Copying intent is not established.

## 18. Kottukkaali (2024)

**Hypothesis:** Mixed / Contested  
**Confidence:** high  
**Review depth:** source-audit  
**Audit status:** reviewed

**Dimensions:** Dharma 2 · Civilization 3 · Rashtra 1 · Itihasa 1 · Parampara 2 · Local Roots 5 · Raksha 1 · Social Dharma 5 · Sacred Regard 1 · Contempt Risk 2

**Why**
- The film is a highly rooted Madurai-region story about a woman whose inter-caste love is reframed by her family as possession and taken to an exorcist; caste, patriarchy and ritual belief are intentionally placed under scrutiny.
- Creator testimony says the story grew from incidents in Vinothraj's own/extended family and from observing similar practices across India, so the ritual critique is deliberate rather than incidental.
- The film criticizes a specific coercive belief/practice and clan/caste control; the evidence does not justify escalating that into a claim of blanket contempt for Hinduism or Tamil culture.

**Narrative Integrity:** `real-incident-generalization`, status `verified`. Vinothraj says family incidents were the starting point and that he generalized after recognizing similar exorcism practices elsewhere. This is disclosed creator framing, not a concealed adaptation delta.

**Fact:** The premise involves lower-caste love, family coercion, a clan deity and an exorcism journey; Vinothraj explicitly connects it to lived incidents.  
**Interpretation:** The film is adversarial toward coercive superstition/patriarchy while remaining ethnographically rooted; therefore Mixed is more accurate than either Neutral or blanket Not Certified.  
**Intent:** The director explicitly says he wanted to examine beliefs he came to regard as foolish/harmful; no evidence supports a broader anti-Hindu intent claim.

## 19. Aranmanai 4 (2024)

**Hypothesis:** Neutral  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Review depth:** source-audit  
**Audit status:** reviewed

**Dimensions:** Dharma 2 · Civilization 3 · Rashtra 1 · Itihasa 1 · Parampara 3 · Local Roots 3 · Raksha 1 · Social Dharma 2 · Sacred Regard 3 · Contempt Risk 0

**Why**
- A commercial supernatural/family-protection story using temple ritual and an Assamese `Baak` water-spirit as genre material; it does not make a sustained national/civilizational argument.
- The climax and protective supernatural framework treat ritual/deity imagery as efficacious within the film rather than as contempt shorthand.
- Sundar C publicly said Assamese Baak folklore supplied the screenplay idea. A critical review argues the film borrows only the shapeshifting/water-spirit hook rather than meaningfully exploring the Assamese tradition; that is a cultural-adaptation limitation, not evidence of hostile representation.

**Narrative Integrity:** `folklore-adaptation`, status `verified`. Baak is a water-associated shapeshifting spirit in Assamese folklore; the film relocates/uses that material in a Tamil horror-comedy template. Critical reporting notes the adaptation is shallow.

**Fact:** Sundar C attributed the story idea to Assamese Baak folklore; folklore sources describe Baak as a water-associated spirit.  
**Interpretation:** This is cross-regional folklore borrowing with limited cultural depth. It is not strong enough for certification and not materially contemptuous enough for rejection.  
**Intent:** Creator intent to use an interesting folklore premise is directly stated; no hostile intent established.
