## 9. Rocketry: The Nambi Effect (2022)

**Hypothesis after audit:** `mixed`  
**Confidence:** high  
**Review depth:** source-audit  
**Audit status:** reviewed

### Why this result
1. The film is strongly India-positive: Nambi Narayanan's scientific ambition is repeatedly framed around strengthening India's space capability, and Madhavan described Narayanan's work as naturally aligning with the country's interest even while rejecting simplistic jingoism.
2. The core injustice is real and material: Narayanan was falsely implicated in the ISRO espionage case, and the film foregrounds the institutional and family cost of that episode.
3. Worldview alignment does not cure source-fidelity problems. Multiple senior former ISRO scientists publicly disputed major career/technical claims attributed to Narayanan by the film, including who sent him to Princeton, an APJ Abdul Kalam episode, his role in cryogenic development, and the claim that the espionage case delayed India's cryogenic programme.
4. Because those disputes concern the biopic's heroic architecture rather than a trivial compression, the appropriate result is Mixed / Contested rather than automatic certification.

### Dimensions
`dharma 3 · civilizationalContinuity 3 · rashtra 5 · itihasa 3 · parampara 2 · localRoots 4 · raksha 4 · socialDharma 4 · sacredRegard 2 · contemptRisk 0`

### Narrative Integrity
**Finding: biographical-credit / technical-history dispute — SUPPORTED**
- **Fact:** Former LPSC director A. E. Muthunayagom, former cryogenic project director E. V. S. Namboothiri, D. Sasikumaran and other former ISRO scientists publicly challenged several film claims, including Sarabhai sending Narayanan to Princeton, Narayanan correcting Kalam, and the extent/timing of Narayanan's role in cryogenic development. They also disputed the claim that his arrest caused India's cryogenic programme delay.
- **Interpretation:** The film substantially concentrates institutional/scientific achievement into its protagonist and therefore should not be consumed as an uncontested record of ISRO technical history.
- **Intent:** Madhavan has described the film as a passion project meant to restore attention to Narayanan's achievements and injustice. The evidence reviewed establishes heroic compression/contested attribution, not an intent to falsify ISRO history.

**Finding: omitted/compressed legal struggle — VERIFIED**
- **Fact:** Madhavan said he omitted large portions of Narayanan's later legal struggle, including details of his Supreme Court fight, because the story had to be compressed.
- **Interpretation:** The film is explicitly selective even by its director's account; omission itself is not ideological distortion but reinforces the need to treat it as an authored biopic rather than documentary history.
- **Intent:** The director openly attributes this to narrative compression.

No material anti-Hindu or anti-India contempt finding survived the adversarial pass.

Conversely, stronger primary documentation showing systematic appropriation of colleagues' achievements could deepen the Mixed result. Both directions were actively searched.

## 10. Ram Setu (2022)

**Hypothesis after audit:** `mixed`  
**Confidence:** high  
**Review depth:** source-audit  
**Audit status:** reviewed

### Why this result
1. The film treats Shri Ram, the Ramayana and Ram Setu with explicit reverence and frames protection of inherited sacred/cultural heritage as a legitimate civilizational good. Akshay Kumar publicly described the production as proudly rooted in Indian history and culture and respectful of beliefs associated with Shri Ram and Ram Setu.
2. Its protagonist's movement from scepticism to belief is not incidental: the plot ultimately presents archaeological-style discoveries as proving a man-made bridge associated with Rama and even reveals the guide AP as Hanuman.
3. The factual layer is much less secure than the spiritual/civilizational layer. The Government of India told Parliament in December 2022 that available satellite indications do not conclusively establish a man-made bridge of the claimed antiquity; in 2026 the ASI also said Ram Setu was not a protected monument of national importance and no such proposal was pending at headquarters.
4. The film therefore aligns strongly with the lens on sacred regard and civilizational continuity, but its dramatized scientific certainty must be surfaced rather than laundered by that alignment. Mixed / Contested best preserves both facts.

### Dimensions
`dharma 5 · civilizationalContinuity 5 · rashtra 3 · itihasa 4 · parampara 5 · localRoots 4 · raksha 2 · socialDharma 2 · sacredRegard 5 · contemptRisk 0`

### Narrative Integrity
**Finding: archaeological certainty exceeds established evidence — VERIFIED**
- **Fact:** The film's story culminates in its archaeologist presenting discoveries that establish Ram Setu as man-made and linked to Rama. In Parliament in December 2022, the Union government said space/satellite evidence shows indications of a structure but does not conclusively establish a man-made bridge of the asserted antiquity. A March 2026 RTI response from ASI's Monument Section said the structure was not protected as a monument of national importance and no such proposal was pending at headquarters.
- **Interpretation:** The film converts a live faith/history/science question into adventure-fiction certainty. That is legitimate fiction, but the certainty should not be presented by this product as settled archaeology.
- **Intent:** The makers openly described the project as respectful of Shri Ram/Ram Setu belief and as based on research. This establishes an affirmative cultural intention, not proof that every scientific proposition is accurate.

**Finding: research/life-story appropriation allegation — DISPUTED**
- **Fact:** Sri Lanka-based historian Ashok Kumar Kainth publicly alleged that the film used his Ramayana research and aspects of his life without permission and said parts were fabricated. The material reviewed establishes the allegation, not an adjudicated finding of copying.
- **Interpretation:** This is relevant originality/source-provenance radar but does not justify treating the film as proven plagiarism.
- **Intent:** Not established.

Searches were run for archaeological claims, anti-science criticism, religious representation complaints, copyright/source allegations and viral 'proof' claims. Later AI-generated underwater videos circulated as supposed real Ram Setu evidence; they are not evidence about the 2022 film and were excluded from the verdict. No social post was used as proof.

Conversely, authoritative evidence disproving specific film claims or an adjudicated provenance finding could deepen the integrity concerns. Both directions were actively searched.
