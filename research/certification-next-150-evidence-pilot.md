# Next 150 Certification — Evidence Pilot

Date: 2026-10-03
Branch: `certification-next-150-hi-ta-te-20261003`
Status: **provisional research only — not resolver-visible**

This pilot establishes the evidence and editorial standard for the 50G / 50H / 50I cycle before bulk dossier production. The labels below are working hypotheses, not final certifications. Each title still needs the full v2 dossier, all 11 probes, Fact / Interpretation / Intent separation, red-team challenge, primary-language verification and publication/research gates.

## Hindi 50G

### Fateh (2025) — provisional: Mixed / Contested

**Evidence reviewed**
- India Today review: https://www.indiatoday.in/movies/reviews/story/fateh-review-sonu-sood-stellar-act-weak-execution-jacqueline-fernandez-vijay-raaz-naseeruddin-shah-2662837-2025-01-10
- Times of India review: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/entertainment/hindi/movie-reviews/fateh/movie-review/117086280.cms

**Fact:** Fateh is an ex-special-operations officer living quietly in Punjab who returns to violence after a young woman from his village is targeted by a cybercrime syndicate. The film explicitly frames cybercrime as a threat to ordinary Indians.

**Interpretation:** The protective / raksha impulse and public-warning function are positive signals. The protagonist’s extremely violent vigilante method is a material counter-signal and should not be collapsed into the film’s anti-cybercrime message.

**Intent hypothesis:** Social-awareness action film that wants the audience to fear cyber exploitation and cheer punishment of predators.

**Red-team challenge:** Does the film meaningfully distinguish justice from revenge, or does spectacle turn extrajudicial violence into the primary endorsed value?

**Next probes:** violence endorsement; police/state legitimacy; victim dignity; any communal or national-security framing; adaptation/true-story claims.

### Azaad (2025) — provisional: Sanghi Certified

**Evidence reviewed**
- Times of India review: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/entertainment/hindi/movie-reviews/azaad/movie-review/117312342.cms
- The Week review: https://www.theweek.in/review/movies/2025/01/17/azaad-movie-review-a-flawed-but-entertaining-film-about-romance-rebellion-and-a-horse.html

**Fact:** The film is set in British India. Govind, a stable hand, becomes connected to the horse Azaad and to rebel Vikram Singh, who opposes British rule. The story culminates around an Ardh Kumbh fair.

**Interpretation:** Anti-colonial resistance, personal courage, loyalty and indigenous public-cultural setting are strong positive civilizational signals. The film is historical fiction, so symbolic alignment must not be mistaken for historical accuracy.

**Intent hypothesis:** Mainstream period adventure using a young man and a horse to dramatize dignity and resistance under colonial rule.

**Red-team challenge:** Are the colonial / zamindari dynamics simplified in a way that creates material historical distortion, and does the Kumbh setting carry substantive sacred respect or merely spectacle?

**Next probes:** historical basis; portrayal of rebellion; colonial-era community representation; sacred-setting treatment; factual claims.

### Hisaab Barabar (2025) — provisional: Sanghi Certified

**Evidence reviewed**
- Scroll review: https://scroll.in/reel/1078270/
- Indian Express review: https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/movie-review/hisaab-barabar-movie-review-madhavans-film-nosedives-every-time-neil-nitin-mukesh-shows-up-9796431/
- Times of India review: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/entertainment/hindi/movie-reviews/hisaab-barabar/amp_ottmoviereview/117465183.cms

**Fact:** An honest railway ticket collector notices a small discrepancy in his bank account and uncovers a much larger banking scam involving crores. He takes on a powerful corrupt banker.

**Interpretation:** Honesty, accountability, civic responsibility and resistance to institutional corruption are clear positive signals. Weak or comic execution does not by itself negate the directional values.

**Intent hypothesis:** Common-man anti-corruption story intended to turn a small personal grievance into a wider public-interest fight.

**Red-team challenge:** Does the story actually defend institutional accountability, or merely substitute one infallible hero for functioning institutions?

**Next probes:** depiction of police/regulators; resolution and consequences; factual plausibility of the banking mechanism; family-duty framing.

## Tamil 50H

### Kudumbasthan (2025) — provisional: Mixed / Contested

**Evidence reviewed**
- Indian Express review: https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/movie-review/kudumbasthan-movie-review-manikandan-anchors-this-funny-and-familiar-tale-that-needed-a-firmer-grasp-on-things-9797424/
- Cinema Express review: https://www.cinemaexpress.com/amp/story/tamil/review/2025/Jan/23/kudumbasthan-movie-review-wins-you-over-with-plenty-of-humour-and-relatable-characters

**Fact:** Naveen carries the financial and emotional burdens of a middle-class household. His marriage to Vennila is inter-caste; his family repeatedly insults the marriage and Vennila. The film also stresses self-respect, integrity, providing for a pregnant wife, parents and friends, and the costs of being the family’s responsible provider.

**Interpretation:** Family responsibility, integrity and perseverance are positive signals. The film’s willingness to show caste prejudice and family dysfunction is not itself a negative signal; the key question is whether harmful behaviour is critiqued, normalised or left morally unresolved. The review evidence suggests meaningful tension rather than a clean one-directional family ideal.

**Intent hypothesis:** Humorous but socially observant family drama about the burdens and contradictions hidden inside the ideal of the `kudumbasthan`.

**Red-team challenge:** Does the ending affirm responsible family duty with reform, or demand self-erasure while leaving Vennila’s disrespect materially uncorrected?

**Next probes:** final family reconciliation; Vennila’s agency; caste framing; treatment of parents; debt/business ethics.

### Aghathiyaa (2025) — provisional: Mixed / Contested

**Evidence reviewed**
- Cinema Express review: https://www.cinemaexpress.com/tamil/review/2025/Feb/28/aghathiyaa-movie-review-a-disjointed-horror-film-that-falls-flat

**Fact:** The film combines Siddha medicine, reincarnation, a Siddhar blessing, patriotism, period history and supernatural fantasy. A modern protagonist investigates a centuries-old mansion and a past conflict.

**Interpretation:** Siddha / Siddhar / reincarnation motifs and patriotic historical framing can be positive civilizational signals when treated respectfully. At the same time, the film reportedly mixes factual-sounding claims with fantasy and paranormal material; that creates an elevated evidence-integrity risk.

**Intent hypothesis:** Civilizational fantasy-horror that uses indigenous medicine, spiritual continuity and patriotism as heroic texture.

**Red-team challenge:** Does the film blur historical, medical or astronomical claims into supernatural fiction in a way that a viewer could reasonably read as factual assertion?

**Next probes:** Siddha claims; named historical figures/events; colonial history; sacred/spiritual treatment; medical and astronomical fact checks.

### Vanangaan (2025) — provisional: Mixed / Contested

**Evidence reviewed**
- Indian Express review: https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/movie-review/vanangaan-movie-review-arun-vijay-effectively-shoulders-a-bala-film-that-feels-compromised-9772142/

**Fact:** Koti, a deaf and non-speaking protagonist, repeatedly intervenes against people who attack or demean vulnerable groups, including trans women, a church priest and visually challenged women. The plot also includes graphic sexual violence and Koti’s brutal retaliatory violence.

**Interpretation:** Protection of vulnerable people and opposition to predation are positive moral signals. The review raises substantial counter-signals: gratuitous violence, insensitive depiction of sexual violence, casual racism and a justice model centred on brute-force vengeance.

**Intent hypothesis:** Bala-style moral melodrama in which an outsider becomes a violent avenger for people the surrounding system fails to protect.

**Red-team challenge:** Does the film honour victims and justice, or does its gaze and retaliatory spectacle undermine the dignity it claims to defend?

**Next probes:** victim agency; narrative endorsement of vengeance; disability portrayal; religion/caste commentary; treatment of police/judiciary.

## Telugu 50I

### Gandhi Tatha Chettu (2025) — provisional: Sanghi Certified

**Evidence reviewed**
- Cinema Express review: https://www.cinemaexpress.com/telugu/review/2025/Jan/24/gandhi-tatha-chettu-review-an-earnest-tale-about-fighting-for-the-present-and-future
- Times of India review: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/entertainment/telugu/movie-reviews/gandhi-tatha-chettu/movie-review/117518806.cms

**Fact:** A 13-year-old village girl named Gandhi, inspired by Mahatma Gandhi and her grandfather, tries to save a tree and her village from an industrialist. The film foregrounds non-violence, self-reliance, heritage, nature and collective good.

**Interpretation:** Ahimsa, swavalamban, stewardship, village community and intergenerational moral instruction are strong positive signals under the project lens.

**Intent hypothesis:** Earnest Gandhian fable connecting the freedom struggle’s moral inheritance to present-day local stewardship.

**Red-team challenge:** Does the film turn historical Gandhi into uncritical hagiography or make specific historical claims that need qualification?

**Next probes:** history claims; industrial-development framing; village economics; environmental facts; treatment of dissenting views.

### Court - State vs A Nobody (2025) — provisional: Mixed / Contested

**Evidence reviewed**
- Cinema Express review: https://www.cinemaexpress.com/telugu/review/2025/Mar/13/court-movie-review-a-skillfully-executed-crowd-pleaser

**Fact:** A young lawyer defends Chandu in a case where the legal system is being misused against him. The film includes caste discrimination, custodial violence and a relationship involving a 19-year-old boy and a 17-year-old girl; the climax criticises misuse of POCSO.

**Interpretation:** Equal justice, defence of the powerless and exposure of abuse of law are strong positive civic signals. The review also flags that the climax oversimplifies the policy rationale and legal complexity around protection of minors. This is a material factual / legal nuance risk, not merely a thematic disagreement.

**Intent hypothesis:** Courtroom crowd-pleaser arguing that powerful people can weaponise protective law and social hierarchy against the weak.

**Red-team challenge:** Can the film’s defence of an individual accused coexist with an accurate account of why age-based protective law exists, or does the climax create a misleading generalisation?

**Next probes:** exact ages/timeline; POCSO legal claims; caste/community language; custodial violence; distinction between case-specific abuse and law-wide critique.

### Baapu (2025) — provisional: Mixed / Contested

**Evidence reviewed**
- Cinema Express review: https://www.cinemaexpress.com/telugu/review/2025/Feb/21/baapu-movie-review-a-sinister-dark-comedy-muddled-by-sentimentality

**Fact:** In a small Telangana town, indebted farmer Mallanna and his family face financial ruin and contemplate exploiting a Rythu Bheema insurance payout in a way that threatens his or his elderly father’s life. The film treats the premise as dark comedy and moral crisis.

**Interpretation:** The film appears to expose rather than celebrate the erosion of family duty and personal morality under severe economic pressure. Its attention to farmer distress is socially substantive, while the family’s contemplated act is an obvious dharmic counter-signal whose narrative treatment needs careful separation from character conduct.

**Intent hypothesis:** Dark social satire about debt, desperation and the point at which survival logic corrodes family ethics.

**Red-team challenge:** Does the film ultimately restore moral accountability and family obligation, or sentimentalise / excuse wrongdoing because the characters are poor and desperate?

**Next probes:** ending; insurance and farmer-policy accuracy; treatment of the grandfather; debt causes; village/caste context; whether corruption is condemned or normalised.

## Pilot takeaway

The pilot supports the planned workflow: **do not infer verdicts from setting or identity markers alone**. The strongest records require film-level directional evidence, and Mixed / Contested should be used where positive civilizational or civic themes coexist with material counter-signals such as vigilantism, legal oversimplification, victim-dignity failures, caste/family contradictions, or factual-risk blending.
